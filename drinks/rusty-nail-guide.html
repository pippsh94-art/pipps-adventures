The Rusty Nail: Scotch & Honey 🥃

Scotch whisky, Drambuie, nothing else — two ingredients, zero distractions

The Rusty Nail Story

The Rusty Nail emerged in the 1950s-60s as a bartender's shorthand for Scotch and Drambuie served together — a combination that had been kicking around since the early 1900s under other names ("B.I.F.," short for "Bloody Idiot Face," was an earlier joke name for the same pairing). The "Rusty Nail" name reportedly came from a bartender who stirred the drink with an actual rusty nail to see if anyone would notice.

It became a Rat Pack staple in the 1960s — Dean Martin's drink of choice — and still reads as an old-school, no-nonsense order. Two ingredients, stirred, served on the rocks. There's nowhere for a bad pour to hide.

The Rusty Nail Formula

  • The Scotch: Blended Scotch is traditional and standard — it's smoother and lets the Drambuie's honey-herbal character come through. A good single malt works too, but go easy on anything heavily peated unless that's the point (see the Smoky Nail below).
  • The Drambuie: A Scotch whisky liqueur sweetened with heather honey and spiced with herbs — this is what turns whisky into a Rusty Nail. There's no substitute; it's the whole drink.
  • The Ratio: Classic is 3:1 Scotch to Drambuie, but it ranges from equal parts (sweeter, more honeyed) to 3:1 (drier, more whisky-forward). Start at 3:1 and adjust to taste.
  • The Method: Built directly over ice and stirred — no shaking, no straining.

The Classic Rusty Nail

This is the whole drink. Get the ratio right and there's nothing else to do.

Ingredients & Ratios

Ingredient Amount Notes
Blended Scotch Whisky 1½ oz Johnnie Walker Black, Famous Grouse, Dewar's 12
Drambuie ½ oz 3:1 ratio — the classic starting point
Lemon Twist 1 (optional) Expressed over the top, not traditional but welcome
Large Ice Cube 1 Slow melt keeps it from going too sweet as it sits

Instructions

  1. Fill the Glass: Add a large ice cube to a rocks glass
  2. Pour: Add Scotch, then Drambuie
  3. Stir: 15-20 seconds, just enough to chill and combine — not a long stir, there's no sugar to dissolve
  4. Garnish: Express a lemon twist over the top if using, then drop it in or skip it entirely

Rusty Nail Variations

1. The Smoky Nail ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Swap in a peated single malt for a Rusty Nail with real backbone — smoke against honey is the whole appeal.

Ingredient Amount Notes
Islay Single Malt 1½ oz Laphroaig 10 or Ardbeg 10 — peat is the point
Drambuie ½ oz Same ratio, but the honey has to work harder against the smoke
Large Ice Cube 1

Instructions: Build and stir exactly as the classic. No garnish needed — the peat smoke is the aromatic.

2. The Dirty Nail ⭐⭐⭐⭐

A float of a second, bolder Scotch on top adds a layered aroma — the first sip hits smoky, the drink settles into honey underneath.

Ingredient Amount Notes
Blended Scotch 1½ oz Base pour, same as classic
Drambuie ½ oz Same ratio
Islay Single Malt (float) ¼ oz Poured gently over the back of a spoon so it sits on top

Instructions: Build and stir the base as the classic, then float the single malt on top last — pour it slowly over an upturned bar spoon so it layers instead of mixing in.

Rusty Nail Pro Tips

  • Don't over-stir: Unlike an Old Fashioned, there's no sugar to dissolve — 15-20 seconds is plenty, just enough to chill.
  • Watch the ratio as it melts: A Rusty Nail gets sweeter as the ice dilutes it, since the honey notes become more prominent relative to the whisky's bite. Drink it while it's cold.
  • Glass matters less than usual: Rocks glass is standard, but this is a forgiving drink — it works in almost anything with ice.
  • Don't substitute other whisky liqueurs: Scotch-based honey liqueurs that aren't Drambuie (there aren't many good ones) will change the drink's identity. This isn't a "whisky and something sweet" formula — Drambuie specifically is the drink.

The Rusty Nail doesn't try to be clever. It's a Rat Pack order from a simpler era of drinking — good Scotch, a honeyed liqueur built specifically to go with it, ice, and nothing to hide behind.
