Mark Twain: Quotes That Hold Up

Samuel Langhorne Clemens (1835–1910)

Lines below are taken from his books, notebooks, speeches, and interviews — not from internet quote farms. Where attribution is shaky, it's called out rather than papered over.

The One You Asked About

Did Twain write: "Most men die at 27, we just wait until they are 72 to bury them"?

No. There is no evidence he wrote or said that. Quote sites (Goodreads, AZQuotes, QuoteFancy) pin the 27/72 wording on him with no book, speech, letter, or notebook behind it.

The older cousin of the line is usually "Many people die at twenty-five and aren't buried until they are seventy-five." That version gets dumped on Benjamin Franklin just as sloppily — Quote Investigator tracked the family of sayings and found no real evidence Franklin said it either. It looks like a newspaper/speech proverb that drifted until the internet glued famous names on it.

Twain did write plenty about dying too early in spirit. The genuine version of that idea, from him, is the undertaker line further down — not the 27/72 meme.

Truth, Talk, and the Herd

"If you tell the truth you don't have to remember anything."
— Notebook, January or February 1894
"When in doubt, tell the truth."
— Following the Equator; Twain later said he invented the maxim
"Truth is the most valuable thing we have. Let us economize it."
— Following the Equator (Pudd'nhead Wilson's New Calendar)
"Whenever you find yourself on the side of the majority, it is time to reform (or pause and reflect)."
— Notebook, 1904
"Reader, suppose you were an idiot. And suppose you were a member of Congress. But I repeat myself."
— Draft manuscript, c. 1881, quoted in Albert Bigelow Paine's biography

Character

"Courage is resistance to fear, mastery of fear — not absence of fear."
— Pudd'nhead Wilson
"Always do right. This will gratify some people and astonish the rest."
— Note to the Young People's Society, Greenpoint Presbyterian Church, 1901
"When angry, count four; when very angry, swear."
— Pudd'nhead Wilson
"Let us endeavor so to live that when we come to die even the undertaker will be sorry."
— Pudd'nhead Wilson's Calendar
"Everyone is a moon, and has a dark side which he never shows to anybody."
— Following the Equator
"Man is the only animal that blushes. Or needs to."
— Following the Equator

Work, Words, and Money

"Never put off till tomorrow what may be done day after tomorrow just as well."
— "The Late Benjamin Franklin," 1870
"Get your facts first, and then you can distort 'em as much as you please."
— Interview with "Luke Sharp," Idler Magazine, 1892
"The difference between the almost right word and the right word is really a large matter — 'tis the difference between the lightning-bug and the lightning."
— Letter to George Bainton, 1888
"Thunder is good, thunder is impressive; but it is lightning that does the work."
— TwainQuotes lightning file (letter/speech corpus)
"A classic is something that everybody wants to have read and nobody wants to read."
— Speech, quoting Professor Winchester's definition
"The lack of money is the root of all evil."
— More Maxims of Mark (Merle Johnson, 1927 compilation from Twain papers)
"I was gratified to be able to answer promptly, and I did. I said I didn't know."
— Life on the Mississippi
"Education consists mainly in what we have unlearned."
— Notebook, 1898

Travel, Death, and Leaving the Porch

"Travel is fatal to prejudice, bigotry, and narrow-mindedness, and many of our people need it sorely on these accounts."
— The Innocents Abroad (1869)
"The report of my death was an exaggeration."
— 1897 reply to a premature obituary (wording varies in press accounts; this is the tightest attested form)

Famous Lines That Are Not Twain

Skip these when they show up with his name attached:

"Twenty years from now you will be more disappointed by the things you didn't do than by the ones you did do." — not Twain (H. Jackson Brown / later self-help).
"The two most important days in your life are the day you are born and the day you find out why." — not Twain.
"History doesn't repeat itself, but it rhymes." — Twain did write "History never repeats itself," but the clever "rhymes" kicker is later folklore, not a sourced Twain sentence.
"Kindness is a language which the deaf can hear and the blind can see." — not a sourced Twain line.
"The man who does not read has no advantage over the man who cannot read." — attribution is shaky; don't treat it as gospel.

Get your facts first, and then you can distort 'em as much as you please.
