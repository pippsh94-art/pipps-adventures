Greg's Thoughts

Working notes — Nov 1, 2026

Two sermons. Same diagnosis. Hollywood worst-case coloring. Gladiator and Fight Club as metaphors for the second half of a working life.

The usable part is smaller than the movies. Keep that. Throw the rest.

The diagnosis
First half of life: build identity, career, a place in the world.
Second half: that fuel dies. Status and title stop charging the battery.
Double squeeze: the org wants cheaper and faster right as you stop wanting to impress it.
People become a line item. Short-term goals, no real care. That is the contract, not a glitch.
Stuff and job are not you. The catalog was never the prize.
Buried anger does not vanish. It leaks if you never look at it.
The Hollywood / worst-case path
Gladiator: they murder the life you built overnight. You are a slave with only old fighting skills.
Fight Club: psychotic break, smash it all, hit bottom, shoot the shadow.
Someone actually ran the civilian version — torch the company, no plan — and called it a wreck.
Useful as caution tape. Useless as a map if you still have people you love, a date on the calendar, and a paycheck clearing.
The third option (the only part that is not theater)
Do not grind harder at a dead game.
Do not blow up the remaining years to prove a point.
Stay professional, not devoted. Take the money. Do not donate extra soul.
Know runway: monthly burn, months covered.
Aim leftover care at health, people, place, the real projects — not the quarter.
Anger at “they don’t care about people” is a value, not a personality defect. Keep the value. Do not spend it on the machine.
What this is not
Not “you’re Maximus in the dirt.”
Not “join Project Mayhem.”
It is: last high-leverage W-2 years while the costume already feels stupid. Use them. Then leave on purpose.

Caring more than management is a bad trade. They have a quarter. You have a life.

Jung — Afternoon of Life is the same problem without the movie ending.

Inspired by two YouTube pieces (same creator) using Gladiator and Fight Club as metaphors — rewrite, not a transcript. No call-to-action, no “smash it all.” The diagnosis stayed. The Hollywood ending did not.
