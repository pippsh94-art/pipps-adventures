Idaho Camping Near Star: Lake Shortlist 🏕️

Eight lakes ranked by drive time from Star, Idaho — close options to the long shots worth the extra miles

Close (2–2.5 hrs)

1. Warm Lake — Shoreline Campground

  • ~2.5 hrs north via Cascade
  • Right on Warm Lake, surrounded by pines
  • Sites fit smaller rigs, 18ft is perfect
  • Less crowded than the big lakes
  • USFS campground, first-come-first-served + some reservable

2. Cascade Lake — Crown Point Campground

  • ~2 hrs north on Hwy 55
  • Beautiful lakeside sites in the trees
  • State park, so reservable on idahostateparks.reserveamerica.com
  • Boat ramp, fishing, the works

3. Deadwood Reservoir

  • ~2.5 hrs northeast via Lowman
  • Remote, less competition for sites
  • Forested, stunning alpine lake
  • USFS sites, most are FCFS

Mid Range (3–3.5 hrs)

4. Redfish Lake — Glacier View & Outlet Campgrounds

  • ~3.5 hrs northeast (Stanley area)
  • Arguably the most beautiful lake in Idaho
  • Surrounded by Sawtooths, tall pines
  • Books up FAST — reservations open 6 months out on Recreation.gov
  • 18ft fits fine at most sites

5. Alturas Lake

  • ~3.5 hrs, just south of Redfish
  • Quieter alternative, equally gorgeous
  • Tall lodgepole pines, clear water
  • USFS, reservable on Recreation.gov

6. Payette Lake — Ponderosa State Park (McCall)

  • ~2.5 hrs north
  • Peninsula campground surrounded by lake on 3 sides
  • Big ponderosa pines, excellent facilities
  • Reserve through Idaho State Parks

Farther But Worth It (3.5–4 hrs)

7. Priest Lake

  • ~4 hrs north
  • Remote, pristine, old-growth cedars
  • Indian Creek & Lionhead campgrounds are great
  • Idaho State Park — reservable

8. Stanley Lake

  • ~3.5 hrs, near Stanley
  • Sawtooth views from camp, lakeside
  • Less insane than Redfish for availability

Booking Tips

  • Recreation.gov — Federal/USFS campgrounds. Reservations open 6 months ahead. Set alerts.
  • Idaho State Parks — parksandrecreation.idaho.gov — reserve up to 9 months out
  • FCFS spots (Warm Lake, Deadwood) — arrive Thursday or early Friday for weekends
  • Dispersed camping — Free, legal on most National Forest land. No amenities but no reservation stress. Tons of spots around Warm Lake, Stanley basin, and McCall areas. Just need to be self-contained.

The Picks

Warm Lake or Deadwood Reservoir if you want easy availability without fighting the reservation game. They're off the beaten path enough that you can usually grab a spot, and they check every box — lake, trees, fits your camper.

Redfish Lake if you want the "wow" factor and can plan ahead.
